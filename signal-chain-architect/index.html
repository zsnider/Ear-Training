Where to start

Set the difficulty to Easy and the chain length to 2. Press New Round — audio will play with a hidden chain of effects applied. Listen carefully, then drag the effect blocks from the palette into the chain slots in the order you think they appear. Press Submit to check.

Increase the chain length to add more effects to identify at once. Harder difficulty settings make each individual effect more subtle, requiring finer listening.

Effect order matters

The order of effects in a signal chain changes the sound — the output of each stage feeds into the next. For example, compressing before distortion produces a different result than distorting before compressing. Listen to how the overall texture builds and what the "entry point" of the processing feels like.

Use Space/D to A/B between the target (processed) signal and the dry (unprocessed) signal to orient yourself. Toggle between your chain and the target using the player controls once you've placed some blocks.

What each effect sounds like

EQ — changes the tonal balance. A lowpass filter removes high-frequency content, making the sound darker and more muffled. A highpass filter removes low-frequency content, making it thinner and brighter. On Easy, the cutoff is dramatic; on Hard it's more subtle.

Compressor — reduces dynamic range. The signal sounds more "controlled" or "squashed," with less difference between loud and quiet moments. Transients (the initial punch of a sound) are softened.

Reverb — adds a sense of space. The sound gains a "tail" — a decay that simulates the reflections of a room. On Easy, the reverb is long and obvious; on Hard it's shorter and drier.

Saturation — adds harmonic distortion. The sound becomes warmer, thicker, and slightly "gritty." On Easy, the drive is heavy and immediately noticeable; on Hard it's a subtle warmth.
