Where to start

Set the difficulty to Easy — only Drive will be active. Press New Round, toggle between Target and Yours, then adjust the Drive knob until the amount of grit and saturation matches. Press Check Answer when you're ready.

Use custom settings to test one parameter at a time or any combinatation of parameters.

What to listen for

Drive — how hard the signal is pushed into distortion. Low drive adds a subtle warmth or "edge"; high drive produces aggressive, heavily clipped grit. Think of it as how much the input signal is amplified before being shaped.

Wet/Dry — blends the distorted signal with the original clean signal. 100% wet is fully distorted; 0% is dry. Blending the two is common in parallel distortion processing to preserve the body of the original while adding edge.

Tone — a post-distortion EQ tilt. Positive values brighten the distorted signal; negative values darken it. Useful for controlling whether the distortion sounds harsh or warm.

Type — the clipping algorithm, each with a distinct character:

Soft Clip — gentle, rounded saturation. Warm and musical, similar to tube amplifiers. The transition into clipping is gradual.

Hard Clip — abrupt, aggressive clipping. The waveform is cut flat at a threshold, producing a harsh, buzzy sound with strong odd harmonics.

Tape — simulates magnetic tape saturation. Warm and slightly compressed-sounding, with a natural smoothness at high levels. A classic studio texture.

Foldback — when the signal exceeds a threshold, it "folds" back on itself rather than clipping. Produces unusual, complex harmonic content that can sound metallic or chaotic.
